From asking to acting

My curiosity grew into something bigger than asking questions. In May 2023, when I was in eighth grade, I co-founded International Children’s Wellness (ICW) with a friend. ICW is a nonprofit that works on cancer prevention and early detection. We started by giving talks about the warning signs of cancer and offering HPV vaccines in rural communities in India, near my family’s village. Later, we noticed that many people still did not get screened, even after hearing our talks. I wanted to know why. When I asked my grandfather, Thata, about it, he told me, “Sometimes it is better not to know.” His answer helped me see that fear could be a barrier, along with a lack of information. That changed how I thought about our approach.

Now we focus on removing barriers, like supporting mobile mammogram vans that bring screenings closer to where people live.

Connections to the textbook

1. Erikson’s identity versus role confusion

During adolescence, people explore their interests, values, and goals as they develop a sense of who they are.

Starting ICW was a big part of figuring out who I am. It brought together my curiosity about science, my family’s ties to India, and my wish to help people. I am still figuring out where those interests will take me. (Riser et al., 2024, Section 10.1)

2. Metacognition in practice

Metacognition includes checking whether your approach is working. When our talks were not leading to more screenings, we stopped, asked what was going wrong, and changed our plan. This connected my curiosity to evaluating a real problem. (Riser et al., 2024, Section 9.3)

My theme

ICW shows how my curiosity could grow into action. I wanted to understand why people were not getting screened, and what I learned changed how we approached the problem.

This connects to plasticity because we did not stay with our first plan. My family’s ties to India and my interest in science were already part of me. Listening to people helped me use those interests in a different way. (Bermudez, 2026)

Family, fear, and access

ICW also showed me how much access matters. Some people do not get screened because getting to a screening is hard, not because they do not care. That connects to my own childhood. Having a park, a library, and family support nearby made it easier for me to explore.

Thata’s answer reminded me that people may see the same situation differently. I had focused on giving information, but his words made me think about how a screening might feel to someone worried about the result.

What I see now

Wanting to help is a starting point. I also need to understand what makes it hard for people to use that help. ICW taught me to listen, question my assumptions, and be willing to change a plan.
