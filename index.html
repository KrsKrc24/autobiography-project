A life span development reflection

About me

I was born in 2008 and have lived in Issaquah, Washington, my whole life. I grew up with my parents and my younger brother, who was born in 2012. My grandparents stayed with us for about six months each year. In my Indian family, visiting the temple and spending time with relatives were regular parts of life.

I have always liked asking questions and figuring things out. Today, I lead International Children’s Wellness (ICW), a cancer prevention nonprofit I co-founded in eighth grade, and I do research in an immunology lab at Fred Hutch. This project follows that curiosity through early childhood, middle childhood, and adolescence.

What I want to learn

I want to understand how my childhood connects to who I am today. Looking back can help me see how my family, culture, and surroundings shaped my interests. I also want to understand how my ways of learning have changed.

My theme

My personal theme is how my curiosity has grown. When I was little, I explored things by touching and looking at them. Later, I used books and science experiments to find answers. Now, I also learn through conversations, research, and thinking about my own behavior.

The course theme I am using is plasticity, which means the capacity to change throughout life. Our class slides explain that people can change, but they build on what came before, including their genes, families, cultures, and experiences. (Bermudez, 2026)

The slides introduce the documentary 49 Up through the saying “Give me the child until he is seven and I will give you the man.” That made me think about what has stayed the same in my life and what has changed. I have been curious since I was little, but I have learned new ways to use that curiosity. (Bermudez, 2026)
