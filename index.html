A life span development reflection

About me

I was born in 2008 and have lived in Issaquah, Washington, my entire life. I grew up with my parents and my younger brother, who was born in 2012. My grandparents also lived with us for about six months every year, so family has always been an important part of my upbringing. Growing up in an Indian household, I was surrounded by cultural traditions, including visiting the temple and spending time with my relatives. One thing that has always defined me is my curiosity. Ever since I was young, I have enjoyed asking questions, exploring unfamiliar things, and understanding how the world works.

My self-reflection goals

By looking back at my life, I hope to understand how my personality, interests, and ways of thinking have developed over time. I want to see how my childhood experiences influenced the person I am today. I also hope to recognize how my family, culture, and environment contributed to my development. Looking at my experiences through psychology concepts will help me better understand why I behaved certain ways at different ages and how I have matured.

The guiding theme

My theme is growing curiosity because curiosity has been a consistent part of my personality throughout my life. As a young child, I was interested in exploring physical objects and understanding my surroundings. During middle childhood, my curiosity expanded into reading books and conducting science experiments. As an adolescent, I became increasingly interested in asking questions and learning from other people. Although the way I express curiosity has changed, my desire to learn and understand new things has remained the same.

OpenStax distinguishes continuous development, which is gradual and cumulative, from discontinuous development, which involves distinct stages. Learning through exploration, then books, then reflective conversations seems to me like a gradual expansion of my ways of learning. Piaget’s stage theory offers another perspective on changes in reasoning. My memories cannot establish which pattern explains development overall. OpenStax, Section 1.2.
