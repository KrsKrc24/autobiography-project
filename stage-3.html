The story

As a teenager, I continued living in Issaquah with my family. Asking questions was still one of my favorite ways to learn.

During a trip to Glacier National Park, I went whitewater rafting. I was interested in what the guide was saying and kept asking questions. After a while, I felt like he was getting frustrated.

The memory is funny to look back on, but it also made me think about how my questions affect other people.

My curiosity also grew into something bigger than asking questions. In May 2023, when I was in 8th grade, I co-founded International Children’s Wellness (ICW) with a friend. ICW is a nonprofit that works on cancer prevention and early detection. We started by giving talks about the warning signs of cancer and offering HPV vaccines in rural communities in India, near my family’s village. Later, we noticed that many people still did not get screened, even after hearing our talks. I wanted to know why. That question changed our whole approach. Now we focus on removing barriers, like supporting mobile mammogram vans that bring screenings closer to where people live.

Connections to the textbook

1. Metacognition

Metacognition means thinking about your own thinking. It includes noticing how you learn and deciding whether your approach is working.

Looking back at the rafting trip, I can see that asking questions helps me learn. I can also think of ways to use that approach better, such as pausing, listening, and checking whether it is a good time to ask more. I used the same kind of thinking with ICW. When our talks were not leading to more screenings, we stopped, asked what was going wrong, and changed our plan. (Riser et al., 2024, Section 9.3)

2. Erikson’s identity versus role confusion

During adolescence, people explore their interests, values, and goals as they develop a sense of who they are.

Starting ICW was a big part of figuring out who I am. It brought together my curiosity about science, my family’s ties to India, and my wish to help people. I am still figuring out where those interests will take me. (Riser et al., 2024, Section 10.1)

3. Emotional intelligence

Emotional intelligence includes recognizing emotions in yourself and others and using that awareness to guide your behavior.

The rafting memory reminds me to notice how another person might be feeling. I am learning to balance my excitement with listening and giving other people space. My guess about the guide’s feelings could have been wrong, so checking is better than assuming. (Riser et al., 2024, Section 10.2)

My theme

My curiosity now includes conversations and thinking about how I learn. This builds on my earlier habits of exploring and reading. The change has been gradual: I still want answers, but I am learning to pay more attention to the people answering.

Curiosity does not explain every part of growing up. Learning to listen and consider other people’s needs is important too.

Erikson describes adolescence as the stage when people build an identity. My identity did not appear all at once, though. ICW pulled together things that had been growing for years: the noticing I did as a little kid, the reading and experiments in middle childhood, and the values I learned from my family. That is why this stage feels like a continuation of the earlier ones, not a fresh start. (Riser et al., 2024, Section 1.2)

Family, culture, and surroundings

My family and cultural traditions still shape what matters to me. At the same time, talking with teachers, classmates, and other adults has shown me that people have different ways of communicating.

Being able to travel gave me a chance to learn from a guide in a new place. That reminds me that opportunities to explore are shaped partly by the resources and experiences available to us.

ICW also showed me how much access matters. Some people do not get screened because getting to a screening is hard, not because they do not care. That connects to my own childhood. Having a park, a library, and family support nearby made it easier for me to explore.

Looking back

I still think asking questions is a good thing. What has changed is that I am learning when to pause. Listening carefully and choosing a good time for a question can help me learn while respecting the other person’s time.
